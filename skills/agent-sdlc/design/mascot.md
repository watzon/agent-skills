# Mascot

Read when the [brand brief](brand.md) calls for a character, or someone asks for a mascot. Decide the logo relationship first ([logo.md](logo.md)).

## When a mascot fits

- The brief's personality is outgoing or playful, and the brand needs to speak or move: onboarding, empty states, social posts, merchandise.
- Not for a reserved or serious brand. If the brief does not call for one, do not add one.
- A mascot still has to identify the brand, not only charm.

## Role

- **Supplementary** in most brands: it appears beside the logo, in lockups and moments, and the logo stands alone without it.
- **Primary** only when the character is the logo (character-first, see [logo.md](logo.md)). Then it needs a simplified head or silhouette for small sizes.

## Design it

1. **Silhouette first.** Filled solid, it is recognizable. Test as for the logo.
2. **Scale.** A full-body version, and a simple head or icon version that passes the logo's small-size and one-color tests.
3. **Expression range.** Plan emotions before the final: happy, neutral, concerned, error, success at least. Expression can come from eyes, posture, or movement; a face is optional.
4. **Range sheet** before the final: full body, the expressions, front and side, and the small version, on one sheet.
5. **Tie it to the logo:** shared proportions, colors, and stroke or corner style.

## Done when

- [ ] A brief line says why the brand has a mascot, and whether it is supplementary or primary.
- [ ] The silhouette and small version pass the logo quality tests.
- [ ] A range sheet with the expressions exists before the mascot is final.
